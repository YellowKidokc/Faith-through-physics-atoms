## One-minute summary

Mike Licona hosts his friend Gary Habermas for a live Q&A about the resurrection. After a brief introduction about Habermas's 50+ years of research, the bulk of the conversation centers on whether **group appearances** of the risen Jesus count as one of Habermas's "minimal facts" and whether skeptical scholars grant them. Habermas argues yes: the earliest Creed in 1 Corinthians 15 lists three group appearances, and critical scholars such as E.P. Sanders, Dale Allison, Gerd Lüdemann, and even John Dominic Crossan allow that groups had experiences they interpreted as seeing Jesus. The pair also discuss the criteria for a minimal fact, the empty tomb, Paul's eyewitness contacts in Jerusalem, the bodily resurrection, and the strongest texts for Jesus's self-understanding.

## Two-minute walkthrough

The video opens with Mike Licona and Gary Habermas reminiscing about their 27-year friendship and Habermas's early teenage habit of collecting 1,600 note cards on the resurrection.

The first major question (from "Paia") asks whether group appearances are part of the minimal facts, given that they are not affirmed by all skeptical scholars. Habermas replies that they are, because:
- A minimal fact requires sufficient data *and* broad scholarly agreement.
- The earliest Creed (1 Cor 15:3–7) contains three group appearances out of five listed encounters.
- Multiple skeptical or non-Christian scholars grant that groups had experiences they took to be appearances of the risen Jesus.

Licona then presses Habermas on what "vast majority" means. Habermas refuses a fixed percentage but says it is far above 50.1% and varies by fact. He clarifies that the empty tomb, despite being well evidenced, has never been one of his six minimal facts.

The conversation moves through several related topics:
- **Paul and eyewitnesses.** Habermas cites Galatians 1:18 and 2 as evidence that Paul interviewed Peter and James in Jerusalem around 35–36 AD, making Paul a link to firsthand testimony.
- **Bodily resurrection.** Licona argues that Paul believed in a physical resurrection, citing 1 Corinthians 15, Romans 8:11, Philippians 3:21, and 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17. He notes that the 1 Cor 15 Creed itself is ambiguous on physical details, but Paul's own view is not.
- **Deity of Christ.** Habermas says Mark 14:61–64 is a stronger text for Jesus's self-understanding than John 8:58, because it records Jesus's affirmation before the high priest and the charge of blasphemy.
- **Hallucinations.** They address the common naturalistic appeal to group hallucinations, noting that most critical scholars reject this as an explanation because group hallucinations are not well documented.

The session ends with pastoral and personal questions: advice on church choice, responses to Bart Ehrman, the distinction between minimal and maximal evidence approaches, and Habermas's closing claim that if Christ is the Son of God who died and rose, Christianity is true.

## Key takeaways

1. **Group appearances are a minimal fact** for Habermas because the earliest Creed lists them and skeptical scholars grant the underlying experience.
2. **"Vast majority" is intentionally not quantified** by Habermas; the threshold is well above simple majority and differs per fact.
3. **The empty tomb is not one of the six minimal facts**, though Habermas thinks it is well evidenced.
4. **Paul provides early access to eyewitness testimony** through his Jerusalem visits and the apostles' certification of his gospel.
5. **Paul believed in bodily resurrection**, even if the 1 Cor 15 Creed is less explicit on the nature of the appearances.
6. **Mark 14:61–64 is treated as a high-water mark** for Jesus's claim to divine status, stronger in Habermas's view than the more famous John 8:58.
