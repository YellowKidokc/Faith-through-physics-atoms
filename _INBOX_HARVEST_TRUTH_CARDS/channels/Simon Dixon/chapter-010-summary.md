## Two-minute walkthrough

Simon Dixon reacts to the Facebook cryptocurrency hearing, calling it a historic moment for finance and cryptocurrency. He notes that Facebook has done what he predicted years ago: entering financial services and challenging the banking system. While Facebook acts as a regulated, hybrid entity, its move will spark global political discussions about crypto, benefiting the entire industry. Dixon emphasizes that Facebook confirmed it will report directly to the IRS, making it a tax collection and KYC tool for the US government. This contrasts sharply with Bitcoin, which has no CEO and cannot be summoned to hearings.

Dixon highlights the separation of US and non-US wallets. The US wallet, Calibra, will be backed by the US dollar and will likely compete with Tether, the current USD-backed stablecoin used by crypto exchanges. Non-US wallets may be backed by other currencies or baskets, creating jurisdiction-specific stablecoins. He sees this as a significant development to watch.

On why Switzerland was chosen for Libra, Dixon says Facebook gave a political answer about neutrality, but the real reason is that US regulation is driving innovation offshore. He believes Facebook is well-equipped to handle regulatory scrutiny, unlike smaller crypto companies. Overall, Dixon views the hearing as net positive for crypto, as it forces regulators and politicians to engage with the topic, ultimately benefiting Bitcoin and decentralized finance.
