## 0. THE DISCOVERY

The corpus does not begin with answers. It begins with questions capable of exposing what an answer would have to contain.

The BFP-DG forward questions Q0–Q14, reverse questions B0–B8, convergence check, and Why-Closure Gate together form the interrogation grammar for the rest of the system. They do not belong to physics, mathematics, history, or theology. They belong to burdens. Their function is to discover which burdens exist and route each burden to the object anatomy capable of carrying it.

```text
QUESTION
→ BURDEN
→ REQUIRED OBJECTS
→ REQUIRED EDGES
→ TESTS
→ ANSWER PACKET
→ RESOLUTION STATE
```

This is the final missing intake layer above the four canonical object types:

```text
QUESTION/BURDEN ROUTER
          ↓
CLAIM · EVIDENCE · PROOF · PROCESS
          ↓
TYPED ANSWER GRAPH
          ↓
OPEN · PARTIAL · CLOSED · FAILED · REFUTED
```

The questions can generate every kind of information the corpus needs. They cannot make an unsupported answer true. Completeness of interrogation and correctness of resolution are separate axes.

# PART I — THE STATUS OF A QUESTION

## 1. A QUESTION IS A BURDEN ROUTER

A question is not a fifth epistemic object parallel to claim, evidence, proof, and process. It does not assert, discriminate, derive, or execute. It requests one or more of those functions and records whether the request has been satisfied.

A question is therefore a first-class **burden record** with:

```yaml
question_id:
raw_question:
source:
scope:
presuppositions: []
exact_answer_space: []
burden_types: []
required_object_types: []
required_edges: []
required_tests: []
answer_packet_ids: []
resolution_state:
unresolved_fields: []
next_discriminating_action:
```

It is first-class because it must be addressable, versioned, corrected, and linked. It is not a fifth object layer because it carries no independent epistemic authority.

## 2. QUESTIONS BELONG TO BURDENS, NOT DISCIPLINES

The wording or source location of a question does not determine its method. “Does God exist?” can contain logical, metaphysical, historical, empirical, phenomenological, moral, and theological burdens. “What is energy?” can contain definitional, mathematical, measurement, historical, and interpretive burdens.

The router decomposes mixed questions until each burden can name:

- what kind of answer would satisfy it;
- which object type must carry that answer;
- which evidence, proof, or process is required;
- what would count against the answer;
- what remains open if the burden cannot be closed.

No discipline owns the question merely because its vocabulary appears in it.

## 3. THE QUESTION IDENTITY RULE

Two wordings are the same question only when they preserve the same answer space and burden. A change in scope, quantifier, presupposition, register, or demanded warrant may create a new question.

For example:

```text
Does an unconditioned ground exist?
Is the unconditioned ground personal?
Is the personal ground loving?
Is the loving ground necessarily triune?
Is the triune ground the God revealed as Father, Son, and Holy Spirit?
```

These are not five phrasings of one question. They are five successive burdens. Closing an earlier burden does not close a later one.

## 3A. NESTED QUESTIONS AND COMPOUND ROOTS

The hard-case trials showed that a content-rich root such as “the specifically Christian Trinitarian God” is a bundle, not one proposition. A compound question must be decomposed into conjunct-level questions with independent verdicts.

Its negation is likewise a family rather than one opposite sentence. For a conjunction `A ∧ B ∧ C`, the countermodel set includes at least:

```text
¬A ∧ B ∧ C
A ∧ ¬B ∧ C
A ∧ B ∧ ¬C
¬A ∧ ¬B ∧ C
¬A ∧ B ∧ ¬C
A ∧ ¬B ∧ ¬C
¬A ∧ ¬B ∧ ¬C
```

The router must therefore generate an exact-negation family, not use “not the whole bundle” as though it identified which load-bearing conjunct failed.

# PART II — THE FORWARD QUESTION GRAMMAR

## 4. Q0 — EXACT EXPRESSION

**Question:** What exactly was said before analysis?

**Purpose:** Preserve the source and prevent clarification from replacing it.

**Produces:** source manifest, raw claim candidate, source hash, speaker-intended question, context, and inherited labels held aside.

**Routes to:** CLAIM for asserted content; EVIDENCE if the raw object is itself a record; PROCESS if the expression specifies an operation.

**Failure state:** `SOURCE_UNPRESERVED` or `QUESTION_DRIFT`.

## 5. Q1 — REFERENT

**Question:** What is being picked out?

Subquestions:

```text
What is the referent?
Is it one or many?
Stable or changing?
Real, possible, imagined, stipulated, or undecided?
```

**Produces:** referent candidates and ontological-status claims.

**Guard:** Naming a referent does not establish its existence.

## 6. Q2 — IDENTITY AND DISTINCTION

**Question:** What makes it this rather than not-this?

**Produces:** identity conditions, distinction conditions, equivalence criteria, and counterinstances.

**Routes to:** CLAIM definitions; PROOF obligations for claimed equivalences; EVIDENCE for empirical identity criteria.

**Failure state:** `IDENTITY_UNDERDEFINED`.

## 7. Q3 — DEPENDENCY FLOOR

**Question:** What is required for the expression to be formulated, coherent, instantiated, known, or true?

The five dependencies must be separated:

```text
FORMULATION dependency
COHERENCE dependency
EXISTENCE dependency
EVIDENTIAL dependency
TRUTH-MAKING dependency
```

**Produces:** dependency claims and `dependsOn` edges.

**Guard:** A condition needed to formulate a claim is not automatically a cause or ground of what the claim describes. The need for a thinker to formulate “number” does not by itself prove that numbers depend ontologically on thinkers.

## 8. Q4 — VARIATION AND INVARIANCE

**Question:** What may vary while the referent remains the same, and what change destroys identity?

**Produces:** state space, invariants, equivalence classes, boundary conditions, and collapse candidates.

**Routes to:** CLAIM and PROOF for formal invariance; EVIDENCE and PROCESS for empirical invariance tests.

## 9. Q5 — CAPABILITY AND OPERATION

**Question:** What can it do, undergo, receive, produce, combine with, preserve, change, or forbid?

**Produces:** operation signature, capability classes, agent/patient relations, conservation claims, and forbidden transitions.

**Guard:** Grammar must distinguish an entity’s operation from an observer’s operation on a representation of that entity.

## 10. Q6 — TRANSITION

**Question:** What connects one valid state to another?

Required fields:

```text
prior state
transition
next state
necessary inputs
sufficient inputs
rule or mechanism
reversibility
cost or loss
```

**Produces:** transition claims, process candidates, and proof obligations.

**Guard:** A rule describing a transition is not necessarily the causal mechanism producing it.

## 11. Q7 — CONSTRAINT AND DOMAIN

**Question:** What limits the possibilities?

**Produces:** admissible and forbidden states, initial and terminal conditions, domain of validity, identity limits, external limits, and kill conditions.

**Routes to:** CLAIM for boundaries; PROCESS for boundary tests; EVIDENCE for observed violations.

## 12. Q8 — CONSEQUENCE AND LICENSE

**Question:** What follows if the expression is granted?

Each consequence must state:

```text
premises
conclusion
inference kind
inference rule or warrant
added requirements
non-consequences often confused with it
```

**Produces:** conclusion CLAIM atoms, premise CLAIM atoms, and PROOF candidates.

**Guard:** “If granted” creates conditional consequences. It does not establish that the premise is true. No arrow without a license.

## 13. Q9 — REPRESENTATION LADDER

**Question:** In what forms can the structure be represented?

Candidate rungs include:

```text
plain language
controlled grammar
logical form
relational or set form
algebraic form
dynamical form
advanced formalism
machine-checkable form
```

**Produces:** representation objects or claims and translation processes.

**Guard:** A proposed formula is not automatically a faithful formalization. Mathematical notation does not confer proof.

## 14. Q10 — PRESERVATION AND LOSS

**Question:** What does each representation preserve, lose, add, merge, or distort?

**Produces:** BRIDGE claims, mapping tables, preserved relations, mandatory loss fields, commutativity tests, and interpretation boundaries.

**Resolution rule:** A representation without a loss account remains `DISCOVERY_INCOMPLETE`.

## 15. Q11 — TRUE-WORLD PROFILE

**Question:** What would reality, the model, or the historical record look like if the claim were true?

**Produces:** predicted consequences separated by register, observable expectations, risky predictions, and potential supporting evidence edges.

**Guard:** A theological consequence is not an empirical prediction unless an observation protocol and discriminating expectation are supplied.

## 16. Q12 — FALSE-WORLD AND RIVAL PROFILE

**Question:** What would reality look like if the claim were false or a rival were true?

**Produces:** exact negations, alternative worlds, live rivals, countermodels, and expected observations under each.

**Guard:** Rivals must be stated in forms their defenders could recognize. A worldview label is not a countermodel until its structure and expected consequences are specified.

## 17. Q13 — DISCRIMINATION

**Question:** What evidence or proof can distinguish the live possibilities?

It routes to:

```text
formal test
empirical test
historical test
phenomenological test
bridge-preservation test
negative control
non-discriminating result
```

**Produces:** EVIDENCE requirements, PROCESS protocols, PROOF obligations, preregistered kill conditions, and discrimination edges.

**Guard:** “The claim explains this” is not enough. The record must say how the observation differs under the claim and named rivals.

### 17.1 Historical and revelatory evidence

Historical/revelatory questions require the complete transmission anatomy: event, observers, witness, testimony, transmission, document, preservation, corroboration, interpretation, and present claim. Revelation may identify a theological referent within its confessed register; public historical identification still requires source-critical argument.

### 17.2 First-person and intersubjective evidence

Consciousness and experience require a register that does not reduce first-person presence to third-person measurement while still permitting intersubjective checks. The packet distinguishes:

```text
first-person occurrence report
behavioral or neural correlate
shared protocol
cross-subject regularity
report reliability
ontology or grounding interpretation
```

Neural dependence can close locally while the grounding question remains open.

## 18. Q14 — EMERGENT ROLE CLASSIFICATION

**Question:** What role did the expression actually play after analysis?

Possible output labels include:

```text
DECLARATION · DEFINITION · AXIOM · BOUNDARY_CONDITION
INFERENCE_RULE · LEMMA · THEOREM · MODEL · INTERPRETATION
BRIDGE · ANALOGY · EMPIRICAL_CLAIM · PREDICTION · PROTOCOL
THEOLOGICAL_REGISTER · OPEN_STATUS
```

**Produces:** classification axes and reconciliation work.

**Guard:** This is the last forward question. Classification is an output, never a premise smuggled back into Q1–Q13.

# PART III — THE REVERSE QUESTION GRAMMAR

## 19. B0–B8 — FROM CONTENT BACK TOWARD POSSIBLE GROUNDS

```text
B0 — What content is actually observed, experienced, encoded, or reported?
B1 — What pattern repeats across the cases?
B2 — What remains invariant across those repetitions?
B3 — What operation or transformation preserves the invariant?
B4 — What constraints are required for that operation?
B5 — What dependencies are required by those constraints?
B6 — What minimal structure could supply those dependencies?
B7 — What candidate ground classes could supply that structure?
B8 — What competing ground classes remain live?
```

Every B-step produces a CLAIM candidate and an edge to its predecessor. Every arrow is typed:

```text
DEDUCTIVE
INDUCTIVE
ABDUCTIVE
STATISTICAL
ANALOGICAL
DEFINITIONAL
COMPARATIVE
UNDERDETERMINED
```

Reverse reconstruction normally returns a set, not a preferred answer:

```text
R(signature) = {candidate ground 1, candidate ground 2, ...}
```

The method may rank candidates through an explicit process. Ranking is not unique recovery.

## 20. THE REVERSE-PASS ANTI-SMUGGLING TEST

Before accepting B6–B8, ask:

- Did the candidate ground already appear in the definitions?
- Was “ground” assumed to require termination?
- Was an infinite regress shown incoherent or merely judged unsatisfying?
- Was brute fact refuted or simply refused as an explanation?
- Were personal properties recovered from the signature, or imported from the intended conclusion?
- Can an impersonal structure generate the same observed invariants?
- Does the candidate explain the evidence better, or only redescribe it?

If these questions remain unanswered, recovery remains `UNDERDETERMINED`.

# PART IV — THE MEETING AND CLOSURE LAYERS

## 21. CONVERGENCE

The top-down discovery signature and bottom-up recovery signature meet without preferred labels at:

```text
IDENTITIES · DISTINCTIONS · RELATIONS · OPERATIONS · DEPENDENCIES
· CONSTRAINTS · INVARIANTS · COLLAPSE CONDITIONS · CONSEQUENCES
```

Possible verdicts:

```text
NO_MATCH
LOCAL_MATCH
PARTIAL_CONVERGENCE
MINIMAL_CONVERGENCE
UNIQUE_RECOVERY
UNDERDETERMINED
```

Minimal convergence means the preferred candidate occurs in the recovered set. Unique recovery means it is the only surviving candidate under a sufficiently complete and preregistered search space. “The alternatives considered in this draft failed” does not prove unique recovery over alternatives not considered.

## 22. THE WHY-CLOSURE QUESTIONS

Every convergence asks why the correspondence occurs:

```text
What is the descriptive reason?
What is the mechanism, if applicable?
What structural constraint produces it?
What boundary conditions select it?
What historical path produced it?
What grounds it?
What purpose explains it, if purposive explanation is admitted?
```

Each answer is tested for restatement and assigned an explanation type. Closure is level-specific. Structural closure does not automatically close causal, grounding, historical, or purposive burdens.

WHY outcomes attach to a specific claim at a specific explanatory level. One packet may simultaneously contain:

```text
WHY_CLOSED  — deductive consequence under an admitted root
WHY_CLOSED  — local causal mechanism
WHY_OPEN    — ultimate grounding
WHY_OPEN    — historical identification
WHY_FAILED  — proposed rival-excluding explanation
```

Legacy `WHY_UNFINISHED` records map to `WHY_OPEN` with an explicit unfinished-inquiry reason; they do not map to `WHY_FAILED`.

## 23. THE ANSWER PACKET

An answer is not one sentence. It is the smallest typed graph that satisfies a question’s registered burdens.

```yaml
question_id:
answer_claim_ids: []
evidence_ids: []
proof_ids: []
process_ids: []
edge_ids: []
countermodel_ids: []
correction_ids: []
convergence_verdict:
why_outcomes: []
burdens_satisfied: []
burdens_unsatisfied: []
resolution_state:
human_ruling:
```

## 24. RESOLUTION STATES

```text
UNOPENED   — the question exists but its burdens have not been decomposed.
ROUTED     — required objects and tests are known.
PARTIAL    — some burdens are satisfied and others remain open.
OPEN       — no present answer discriminates the live possibilities.
CLOSED     — all registered burdens at the named level are satisfied.
FAILED     — the proposed answer did not satisfy its burden.
REFUTED    — a proof or valid falsifying edge defeats the answer as stated.
SUPERSEDED — a later question or answer packet replaces current use without deletion.
```

`CLOSED` always names its level and scope. No answer is simply “proved” across all registers.

Every answer produces further questions wherever a dependency, loss, rival, assumption, boundary, or interpretation remains unresolved. The grammar is therefore recursively complete in form without claiming epistemic omniscience.

## 26A. NATIVE → BRIDGE → IDENTIFICATION

Every answer packet is additionally partitioned across three governance layers:

| Governance layer | Question | Required warrant |
|---|---|---|
| **NATIVE** | What does the domain’s own mathematics, evidence, history, or doctrine establish? | theorem, experiment, source, protocol, uncertainty, or native theological warrant |
| **BRIDGE** | What structure is preserved when moving between registers? | explicit mapping, direction, grade, losses, rival comparison, failure condition |
| **IDENTIFICATION** | What does Christian theology say the preserved structure means or names? | Scripture, doctrine, philosophical argument, historical/revelatory evidence |

No answer may jump directly from a native result to a Christian identification without an addressed bridge. Native equations retain authority in their own domains. A bridge is a wrapper or correspondence, not a replacement for the native object.

## 26B. THE SEVEN EVALUATION BURDENS

Before a unification answer can close, the packet asks:

1. **Shared structure:** What exact invariant or constraint grammar is shared?
2. **Native preservation:** Do accepted native results survive within their scope?
3. **Typed bridge:** Are inputs, outputs, units, assumptions, direction, preservation, and loss declared?
4. **Parameter accounting:** Are all free parameters, normalizations, thresholds, and interpretation rules counted?
5. **Novel risk:** What dated prediction, discriminating retrodiction, or new test is risked?
6. **Negative control:** What nearby or randomized alternative fails, and why?
7. **Rival symmetry:** Were serious alternatives run through the same tests and grades?

An answer that omits one of these is not rejected automatically when the burden is inapplicable, but applicability must be ruled explicitly.

# PART VI — APPLICATION TO THE FOUR BFP-DG EXEMPLARS

## 27. THE MARK

The questions successfully expose the performative-distinction burden, the performer/medium issue, representation losses, countermodels, and closure claim. The answer packet must separate:

```text
CLAIM: distinction is performed in any assertion or denial.
PROOF: the bounded performative-contradiction argument.
CLAIM: the act creates two sides.
OPEN: whether the performer is ontologically co-primitive or only required for formulation.
BRIDGE: relations from mark to bit, logic, physics, consciousness, and theology.
```

The performative argument may close denial of distinction as a condition of discourse. It does not by itself prove every proposed ontology of the unmarked state, classical excluded middle, physical symmetry breaking, or divine self-differentiation.

## 28. NUMBER

The questions expose distinction, repeatability, iteration, successor, axioms, models, and ontology. The answer packet must separate:

```text
CLAIM/PROOF: a natural-number object can be generated under specified initial object,
             successor, closure, and induction conditions.
BRIDGE:      repeated marking corresponds to successor structure.
OPEN:        Platonism, nominalism, formalism, structuralism, or another ontology.
```

Repeatable distinction alone does not yet prove the Peano axioms, infinity, induction, non-circular succession, or that every natural number exists. Those are named added burdens. The Mark-to-Number bridge can be strong without being mislabeled a deduction from one premise.

## 29. GOD

The questions expose several distinct steps that the compressed word “God” otherwise hides:

```text
Q-G1: Does explanation require an unconditioned ground?
Q-G2: Is infinite regress impossible, explanatorily incomplete, or merely unsatisfying?
Q-G3: Must the ground be personal?
Q-G4: Must the personal ground be loving?
Q-G5: Does perfect love require internal plurality?
Q-G6: Does internal plurality uniquely require three persons?
Q-G7: Does that structure identify the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit?
Q-G8: What role does historical revelation play in the identification?
```

These questions can produce every necessary answer object. They also prevent one closed burden from laundering the next. On the present execution’s own admissions, unique recovery fails and the Trinity remains WHY_OPEN at full identification. Therefore “personal ground capable of love is WHY_CLOSED” must remain a candidate conclusion until the rival and hidden-premise burdens are separately ruled.

## 30. SCIENTIFIC METHOD

The questions expose observation, regularity, hypothesis generation, prediction, testing, public error correction, replication, and revision. The answer packet must distinguish:

```text
PROCESS: a family of scientific inquiry methods.
EVIDENCE: historical and present successful runs.
CLAIM: the method is reliable within named domains and conditions.
OPEN: scientific realism versus instrumentalism and the uniqueness of one method.
```

Pattern, consciousness, and logic do not deductively force the modern scientific method without added norms: empirical access, induction or updating rules, public criticism, repeatability where applicable, and a preference for error correction. The method’s success supports it; it does not make every alternative inquiry method self-defeating.

## 30A. THE GRAMMAR’S CHARACTER AS AN IDENTIFICATION TEST

The mathematical-character document proposes a repeated pattern—claim, structural excess, addressed beyond—and associates theorem behaviors with character terms such as inexhaustibility, faithfulness, justice, identity, covenant, sovereignty, and abundance.

Under this canon, that material routes as:

```text
NATIVE:         exact mathematical theorem and formally established behavior
BRIDGE:         mapping from that behavior to a character predicate, with losses and rivals
IDENTIFICATION: Christian claim that the character coheres with or is grounded in Logos
```

The native theorem does not prove the character word, and the character word does not uniquely identify God. The recurring structure can be a serious bridge candidate only after label removal, rival descriptions, permutation, loss accounting, and the Why-Gate.

Its strongest legitimate question is not “Do these theorem names prove God?” but:

> Does the independently recovered invariant behavior display a stable character signature, and does the Christian Logos account explain that signature better than live rivals under the same burdens?

That question can now generate a complete answer packet without deciding itself in advance.

# PART VII — NON-NEGOTIABLE GUARDS

## 31. QUESTION GUARDS

- A question may expose a burden; it cannot satisfy its own burden.
- A complete questionnaire is not a complete proof.
- The answer space is stated before the preferred answer is graded.
- Presuppositions are atoms to test, not invisible gifts to the answer.
- Every “must” creates a proof obligation.
- Every “best explanation” creates a rival-comparison process.
- Every “demonstrated everywhere” creates an independence and scope question.
- Every cross-register move creates a bridge with mandatory loss.
- Every closure names its level.
- Every unresolved burden returns `OPEN`, not the desired theology.

## 32. ANSWER GUARDS

- A CLAIM answers what is asserted.
- EVIDENCE answers what discriminates.
- PROOF answers what follows under premises.
- PROCESS answers what was run and what it can distinguish.
- A BRIDGE answers how registers correspond, never whether one proves the other.
- A CORRECTION answers how the graph learned from an error.
- No object answers a burden outside its native authority.

## 33. THE PROVE-EVERYTHING CORRECTION

The strong form of the discovery is not:

> These questions prove everything we have.

The defensible and more powerful form is:

> **These questions can expose every kind of burden our claims must satisfy, generate every object needed to answer those burdens, and prevent an answer from being called proved until the appropriate evidence, derivation, process, bridge, rivals, and closure conditions are present.**

That is not a retreat. It is what turns the framework from a universal assertion machine into a universal accountability grammar.
